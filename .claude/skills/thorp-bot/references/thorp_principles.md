# Thorp's principles for betting and investing

Foundation for the agent, from Edward O. Thorp, *A Man for All Markets* (2017; read here via
the Bookey chapter summary and quotes), plus his Kelly papers where noted. Quotes are
from the book unless marked.

## 1. Only bet when you have a real, measurable edge
- Thorp went to Las Vegas "with a strong belief in playing games where he had an edge" (ch. 4).
  He played basic strategy at a known 0.62% house edge to learn, with small bets, and
  only bet seriously once card counting gave a proven player edge.
- Betting systems cannot create an edge: "with truly random outcomes, no betting system
  could overcome the casino's advantage" (ch. 9, on the Labouchère). Progressions,
  martingales, take-profit rules and parlays change the shape of results, not the expected value.
- He proved edges first, then bet: the IBM 704 calculations came before the casino test,
  and "my system performed at the tables just as the theory predicted" (ch. 5).
- "I decided to see for myself" / "my habit of checking it out for myself" (ch. 5, 11). Verify
  claims, including your own, against data.
- To beat a market, "identify a statistically significant edge … rather than relying on
  luck" (ch. 26). The question is not "is it efficient?" but "in what ways and to what
  extent is the market inefficient, and how can we exploit this?" (ch. 19).

## 2. Size bets by the edge: the Kelly criterion
- "The bigger the edge, the larger the bet; the smaller the risk, the larger the bet" (ch. 28).
- The Kelly criterion "suggests optimal betting amounts based on one's edge and odds,
  promoting strategies that minimize total loss and maximize growth" (ch. 28 review).
  For a bet paying net odds b with win probability p: f* = (b·p − q) / b.
- In blackjack he varied his bet with the count, betting more only when the remaining deck
  favoured him, using "a carefully regulated betting system to manage risk" (ch. 5).
- Full Kelly maximises long-run growth but is volatile. In his Kelly papers (*The Kelly
  Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting and the Stock Market*, 1997/2006), Thorp notes that
  betting more than twice Kelly gives negative growth however good the edge, and that
  fractional Kelly (half or less) gives up a little growth for much smaller drawdowns.
  It's the sensible choice when the probability estimate is uncertain, which it almost
  always is. Half Kelly keeps about 75% of the growth.

## 3. Overbetting is the fatal mistake
- "Betting too much...can be ruinous" (ch. 11). Long-Term Capital Management is the example.
- "Even though I was right in my economic analysis I hadn't properly evaluated the risk of
  too much leverage" (ch. 11, silver).
- "The lesson of leverage is this: Assume that the worst imaginable outcome will occur and ask
  whether you can tolerate it" (ch. 28).
- "If you bet against it too early you can be ruined in the short run even though you are
  right in the long run" (ch. 29). Survive first.
- His funds "analyzed and incorporated tail risk" (ch. 14) and made "reducing risk a central
  feature" (ch. 12). He cut a short-sale order in half during the 1987 crash to cap exposure (ch. 15).

## 4. Discipline and temperament matter as much as math
- "An academic understanding is not enough. You need to think quickly, be disciplined enough
  to follow the system, and have a suitable temperament" (ch. 10).
- Take it "one step at a time, adding a new difficulty only after I became comfortable" (ch. 7).
  Scale stakes up only after the method is proven at small size.
- Avoid anchoring to entry prices or past losses (ch. 11, Electric Autolite). Don't chase
  losses, and don't treat sunk costs as a reason to bet.
- "Cognitive dissonance... where you want to believe something enough that you simply reject
  any information to the contrary" (ch. 17). Scrutinize the bets you like most.
- "If you are not sure, then it probably isn't" (ch. 26). When the edge is unclear, pass.

## 5. Edges get taken away
- Casinos changed rules, reshuffled early and barred counters once the edge was public
  (ch. 7–8). Casinos removed the baccarat natural 8/9 side bets after Thorp beat them (ch. 10).
  Expect sportsbooks and DFS apps to limit winners, and edges to fade as others find them.
- "Bigger stakes attracted bigger thieves" (ch. 18). Check the counterparty: unlicensed venues,
  void rules, withdrawal risk.

## 6. Stay within your circle of competence
- "Focus on investments well within your knowledge and ability to evaluate, your 'circle of
  competence'" (ch. 26).
- Early, accurate information and superior analysis are the legitimate sources of edge (ch. 26).

## 7. Compounding, and what money is for
- "Over a sufficiently long time, compound growth at a small rate will vastly exceed any rate
  of arithmetic growth" (ch. 23). Kelly is about compounding a bankroll, not one big score.
- Pay off high-interest debt before betting or investing (ch. 23).
- "Understanding why casinos usually win might help us avoid gambling and teach us to limit
  our losses to their entertainment value" (ch. 30). Without an edge, it's entertainment, so
  size it like entertainment.
- "Time is worth more to me than the extra money" (ch. 19); "the knowledge that I've got
  enough" (ch. 17).
